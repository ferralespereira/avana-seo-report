[ES-1] THE FAQ STATES THE WRONG TRIGGER FOR THE FILING DEADLINE, AND
       CONTRADICTS THE ARTICLE BODY
Severity: CRITICAL
Location: FaqEntities.xml - ES FAQ 10
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CURRENT FAQ TEXT:
  "Por lo general, usted no tiene mas de dos anos para presentar una demanda
   desde la fecha en que ocurrio la negligencia medica."

CURRENT ARTICLE TEXT (article.cshtml line 54) - says something different:
  "el plazo de prescripcion para los casos de malas practicas medicas en
   Florida es de dos anos despues del descubrimiento de los danos."

WHAT THE STATUTE SAYS - Fla. Stat. s. 95.11(5)(c), verbatim:
  "An action for medical malpractice shall be commenced within 2 years from the
   time the incident giving rise to the action occurred or within 2 years from
   the time the incident is discovered, or should have been discovered with the
   exercise of due diligence; however, in no event shall the action be commenced
   later than 4 years from the date of the incident"

THE PROBLEM:
  The clock runs from the incident OR from discovery - whichever applies. The
  FAQ states only the incident date. In surgical cases the discovery rule is
  frequently the one that saves the claim: a retained sponge or instrument can
  go undetected for years, and nerve or organ damage is routinely written off as
  normal recovery.

  A reader whose injury surfaced 3 years after surgery reads FAQ 10, concludes
  the claim died at year 2, and never calls. That claim may well have been
  viable under the discovery rule.

  The page also contradicts itself: FAQ says "from the date the negligence
  occurred", article says "after discovery of the damages". Two different
  answers to the highest-stakes question on the page.

FIX: Rewrite FAQ 10 to state both triggers plus the 4-year outer limit, and
  align it with the article. Cite s. 95.11(5)(c).

[ES-2] "ALL DOCTORS AND HOSPITALS HAVE MALPRACTICE INSURANCE" - FALSE IN FLORIDA
Severity: CRITICAL
Location: FaqEntities.xml - ES FAQ 6
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CURRENT FAQ TEXT:
  "Todos los medicos y los hospitales tienen seguro de mala praxis medica,
   precisamente para compensar a las personas que danan por algun error que
   cometen."

WHAT FLORIDA LAW ACTUALLY PROVIDES - Fla. Stat. s. 458.320:
  Physicians must demonstrate financial responsibility, but insurance is only
  one of the accepted methods (escrow account and letter of credit are the
  others), AND the statute contains express exemptions permitting a physician
  to carry no coverage at all - the practice known as "going bare".

  A physician who goes bare must post a notice reading:
    "YOUR DOCTOR HAS DECIDED NOT TO CARRY MEDICAL MALPRACTICE INSURANCE."

  A second exemption covers physicians with 15+ years of practice working
  limited hours, whose posted notice reads:
    "YOUR DOCTOR MEETS THESE REQUIREMENTS AND HAS DECIDED NOT TO CARRY MEDICAL
     MALPRACTICE INSURANCE."

THE PROBLEM:
  Florida is one of the states where going bare is both lawful and common. The
  page tells a Miami reader the opposite of the local rule, on a point that
  directly determines whether a judgment is collectible. This is the single
  worst factual claim on the page: it is not merely incomplete, it is wrong in
  the specific jurisdiction the page targets.

  It also cuts against the firm: whether a defendant carries coverage is
  exactly the kind of thing a claimant needs a lawyer to investigate, and the
  FAQ tells them not to worry about it.

FIX: Replace with an accurate statement - most but not all Florida physicians
  carry coverage; Florida permits going bare under s. 458.320 with posted
  notice; identifying every responsible party and every available policy is
  part of the case investigation.

[ES-3] THE 4-YEAR STATUTE OF REPOSE IS MISSING ENTIRELY
Severity: CRITICAL

  and say plainly that the repose clock has been running since the day of the
  surgery.

   que involucren fraude, ocultacion o representacion falsa intencional"

[ES-4] Fla. Stat. s. 768.21(8) - THE "FREE KILL" LAW IS NOT MENTIONED
Severity: HIGH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STATUTE TEXT, verbatim:
  "The damages specified in subsection (3) shall not be recoverable by adult
   children and the damages specified in subsection (4) shall not be
   recoverable by parents of an adult child with respect to claims for medical
   negligence as defined by s. 766.106(1)."

  "Adult child" means 25 or older - s. 768.18(2) defines "minor children" as
  children under 25, notwithstanding the age of majority.

  Effect: in a medical malpractice wrongful death claim, adult children of a
  deceased parent, and parents of a deceased adult child, cannot recover
  noneconomic damages (pain and suffering) at all. Florida is the only state
  with this provision.

CURRENT LEGISLATIVE STATUS - VERIFIED AS OF 2026-08-03: STILL IN FORCE.
  2025-05-29  HB 6017 (repeal) passed both chambers, VETOED by Gov. DeSantis,
              citing the absence of a cap on noneconomic damages
  2026-01-15  HB 6003 (repeal) passed the House 88-17
              Senate companion SB 1700 never received a committee hearing and
              died when the session adjourned
  => s. 768.21(8) has NOT been repealed.

THE PROBLEM:
  The page repeatedly invokes death - "pueden costarle la salud e incluso la
  vida", "potencialmente fatales", "lesiones o muerte" - and then lists
  recoverable damages including "Dolor y sufrimiento", with no warning that
  Florida law may bar precisely those damages for the family members most
  likely to be reading. A bereaved adult child reading this page is given a
  materially incomplete picture of their own rights.

  Accuracy caution: many competitor pages currently state this law was
  "repealed in 2025". That is FALSE - it was vetoed. Getting the status right
  is a verifiable accuracy edge.

Location: article.cshtml line 54

CURRENT PAGE TEXT:

Directionally correct, but it omits the two gates that actually decide whether

  (1) Fla. Stat. s. 766.203(2) - CORROBORATING EXPERT OPINION, verbatim:
      "Corroboration of reasonable grounds to initiate medical negligence
       litigation shall be provided by the claimant's submission of a verified
       written medical expert opinion from a medical expert as defined in
       s. 766.202(6), at the time the notice of intent to initiate litigation

      -> A hard gate. No expert opinion, no case. In a surgical claim that

      "A suit may not be filed for a period of 90 days after notice is
       delivered to any prospective defendant."
      and: "upon mailing of the notice of intent to initiate litigation...the
       statute of limitations is tolled as to all prospective defendants",
       remaining tolled during the 90-day period and any stipulated extension.
      -> The tolling point is the most reassuring fact available to an anxious
         reader watching a 2-year clock, and it is absent.

FIX: Walk the reader through it - investigation, expert opinion, notice of
  intent, 90-day screening, tolling, then filing.

[ES-6] NO MENTION THAT FLORIDA'S NONECONOMIC DAMAGE CAPS ARE UNENFORCEABLE
Severity: HIGH
Location: article.cshtml lines 56-66 (damages section)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FINDING:
  Fla. Stat. s. 766.118 still PRINTS caps on noneconomic damages, but they have
  been struck down:
    - Estate of McCall v. United States, 134 So. 3d 894 (Fla. 2014) - wrongful
      death noneconomic cap held unconstitutional under the Florida
      Constitution's equal protection guarantee.
    - North Broward Hospital District v. Kalitan, 219 So. 3d 49 (Fla. 2017) -
      extended that holding to personal injury (surviving patient) caps.
  => Florida is a no-cap jurisdiction for medical negligence noneconomic
     damages as of 2026.

THE PROBLEM:
  This is strongly favorable to the reader and the page says nothing. Worse,
  the caps remain in the printed statute, so a reader who looks up s. 766.118
  on their own will reach the WRONG conclusion about their own case. The page
  should preempt that.

[ES-7] FAQ 9 MISSTATES THE STANDARD OF CARE
Severity: MEDIUM
Location: FaqEntities.xml - ES FAQ 9
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CURRENT FAQ TEXT:
  "usted debe demostrar que el profesional de la medicina no actuo con un alto
   grado de competencia y tendra que probar que el error que se cometio es uno
   que no se haria por un medico prudente en las mismas circunstancias."

WHAT THE STATUTE SAYS - Fla. Stat. s. 766.102(1), verbatim:
  burden: "the claimant shall have the burden of proving by the greater weight
  of evidence that the alleged actions of the health care provider represented
  a breach of the prevailing professional standard of care"
  definition: "that level of care, skill, and treatment which, in light of all
  relevant surrounding circumstances, is recognized as acceptable and
  appropriate by reasonably prudent similar health care providers"

THE PROBLEM:
  "Un alto grado de competencia" sets the bar higher than the law does and is
  discouraging to a reader assessing their own case. The standard is not "a
  high degree of competence" - it is the level of care recognised as acceptable
  by reasonably prudent SIMILAR providers, proved by the greater weight of the
  evidence. The FAQ's second clause ("un medico prudente en las mismas
  circunstancias") is much closer and should carry the sentence.

PROCESS NOTE - A FALSE ALARM I CAUSED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The first run of this audit reported HTTP 000 for ALL 13 external URLs, which
would have read as "every external link is dead". IT WAS MY BUG. The URL list was
written by Python in text mode on Windows, so the file got CRLF endings; bash
"read -r" then left a trailing 
 on every URL and curl could not resolve them. A
single hand-typed curl to the same URL returned 200, which is what exposed it.
LESSON: when a link check reports EVERYTHING broken, suspect the harness before
the links. Strip 
 (tr -d) when feeding Python-written lists into bash loops.
