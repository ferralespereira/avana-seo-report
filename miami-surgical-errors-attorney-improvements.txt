[C-1] THE 4-YEAR STATUTE OF REPOSE IS MISSING ENTIRELY
Severity: CRITICAL (highest-impact error on the page)
Location: En/article.cshtml line 40 ; Es/article.cshtml line 54
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CURRENT PAGE TEXT (EN):
  "Additionally, the statue of limitations on medical practice in Florida is
   two years after the discovery of the damages."

CURRENT PAGE TEXT (ES):
  "Ademas, el plazo de prescripcion para los casos de malas practicas medicas
   en Florida es de dos anos despues del descubrimiento de los danos."

WHAT THE STATUTE ACTUALLY SAYS (Fla. Stat. s. 95.11, med-mal subsection):
  "An action for medical malpractice shall be commenced within 2 years from the
   time the incident giving rise to the action occurred or within 2 years from
   the time the incident is discovered, or should have been discovered with the
   exercise of due diligence; however, in no event shall the action be commenced
   later than 4 years from the date of the incident or occurrence out of which
   the cause of action accrued..."

THE PROBLEM:
  The page states ONLY the 2-year discovery rule. It never mentions the absolute
  4-year outer limit measured from the date of the incident. A reader whose
  injury surfaces in year 5 would conclude from this page that they still have a
  viable claim. They do not - the claim is extinguished by the repose period
  regardless of discovery.

RISK: Reader detrimental reliance. Also an E-E-A-T/accuracy signal problem: AI
  engines cross-check statutory claims, and an incomplete SOL statement is a
  known trust-demotion trigger for YMYL legal content.

FIX REQUIRED: State both periods explicitly and distinguish limitations from
  repose. Recommend a dedicated "Deadline" section with a clear 2-year / 4-year /
  7-year / minor-exception breakdown.

[C-2] THE EIGHTH-BIRTHDAY EXCEPTION IS MISCHARACTERIZED
Severity: CRITICAL
Location: En/article.cshtml line 41 ; Es/article.cshtml line 55
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CURRENT PAGE TEXT (EN):
  "The exceptions of this would include claimants under the age of eight and an
   extension of a maximum 7 years for cases that involve fraud, concealment or
   intentional misrepresentation by a prospective defendant health care
   provider."

CURRENT PAGE TEXT (ES):
  "Las excepciones incluyen demandantes menores de ocho anos..."

WHAT THE STATUTE ACTUALLY SAYS:
  "...except that this 4-year period shall not bar an action brought on behalf of
   a minor on or before the child's eighth birthday."
  (The same eighth-birthday carve-out is repeated for the 7-year fraud ceiling.)

THREE DISTINCT ERRORS:
  (a) The page presents this as an exception to the 2-YEAR limitations period.
      It is not. It is an exception to the 4-YEAR REPOSE period (and to the
      7-year fraud ceiling).
  (b) "Claimants under the age of eight" is imprecise. The statute turns on
      whether the action is brought on or before the child's eighth birthday -
      i.e. the child's age at FILING, not a general class of claimant.
  (c) Because the page never introduced the 4-year repose at all (see C-1), the
      exception is left dangling with nothing to except.

FIX REQUIRED: Rewrite as an exception to the repose periods, phrased around the
  filing deadline relative to the child's eighth birthday.

[C-3] THE 7-YEAR FRAUD PROVISION IS DESCRIBED BACKWARDS
Severity: CRITICAL
Location: En/article.cshtml line 41 ; Es/article.cshtml line 55
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CURRENT PAGE TEXT (EN): "an extension of a maximum 7 years"
CURRENT PAGE TEXT (ES): "una extension de hasta 7 anos"

WHAT THE STATUTE ACTUALLY SAYS:
  Where "fraud, concealment, or intentional misrepresentation of fact prevented
  the discovery of the injury the period of limitations is extended forward
  2 years from the time that the injury is discovered or should have been
  discovered with the exercise of due diligence, but in no event to exceed
  7 years from the date the incident giving rise to the injury occurred."

THE PROBLEM:
  The page reads as though fraud grants the claimant a 7-year extension ADDED to

  discovery, and 7 years from the incident date is the absolute CEILING on that
  extension. The page inverts a cap into a bonus.

FIX REQUIRED: Present 7 years as an outer ceiling, not an added term.

[C-5] PRESUIT PROCESS DESCRIBED TOO THINLY TO BE USEFUL

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CURRENT PAGE TEXT (EN):
  "Before filing your claim you would have to provide a notice and go through a
   presuit screening."

WHAT IS OMITTED - the two gates that actually decide whether a claim survives:

  (1) Fla. Stat. s. 766.203 - CORROBORATING MEDICAL EXPERT AFFIDAVIT
      Before issuing the notice of intent, the claimant must investigate to
      establish reasonable grounds that a named defendant was negligent and that
      the negligence caused injury. Corroboration must be provided by
      "submission of a verified written medical expert opinion from a medical
      expert as defined in s. 766.202(6), at the time the notice of intent to
      initiate litigation is mailed."
      -> This is a hard gate. No affidavit, no case. The page never mentions it.

  (2) Fla. Stat. s. 766.106 - 90-DAY PRESUIT SCREENING PERIOD
      "A suit may not be filed for a period of 90 days after notice is delivered
      to any prospective defendant." During that window the defendant/insurer
      must conduct a liability review. THE STATUTE OF LIMITATIONS IS TOLLED
      during the presuit period and any stipulated extension. On termination of
      negotiations in an extended period the claimant has 60 days or the
      remainder of the SOL, whichever is greater, to file.
      -> The tolling point is the single most reassuring fact for an anxious
         reader and it is absent.

FIX REQUIRED: Add a real presuit walkthrough - investigation, expert affidavit,
  notice of intent, 90-day screening, tolling, then filing.

[C-6] DAMAGES SECTION OMITS THE TWO FLORIDA-SPECIFIC FACTS THAT MATTER MOST
Severity: HIGH
Location: En/article.cshtml lines 42-50 ; Es/article.cshtml lines 56-66
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  [C-6a] Fla. Stat. s. 768.21(8) - THE "FREE KILL" LAW  *** STILL IN FORCE ***

    WHAT IT DOES: Bars adult children age 25+ and the parents of an adult
    decedent from recovering NONECONOMIC damages (pain and suffering) in a
    wrongful death action arising from medical malpractice. Enacted 1990.
    Florida is the only state with this provision.

    LEGISLATIVE STATUS - VERIFIED TIMELINE:
      2025-03-26  HB 6017 (repeal) passes the House 104-6
      2025-05-01  HB 6017 passes the Senate 33-4
                  (floor amendment adding a $1M damage cap failed 18-19)
      2025-05-29  Gov. DeSantis VETOES HB 6017, citing the absence of damage
                  caps and physician-recruitment concerns
      2025-06-05  House declines to override the veto
      2026-01-15  HB 6003 (repeal) passes the House 88-17
                  Senate companion SB 1700 never received a committee hearing
                  and died when the session adjourned
      AS OF 2026-07-30: s. 768.21(8) HAS NOT BEEN REPEALED. Still governing law.

    WHY THIS IS A PAGE DEFECT: The page repeatedly references surgical errors
    that "cost the patient their life," "potentially fatal" outcomes, and
    "injuries and death in a surgical procedure" - then lists recoverable
    noneconomic damages with no mention that Florida law may bar exactly those
    damages for the surviving family members most likely to be reading. This is
    the single largest substantive omission after C-1.

    GEO VALUE: Extremely high. This is a live, contested, frequently-queried
    Florida-specific issue with a clean verifiable timeline. Very few competitor
    pages carry the correct current status (many still say "repealed in 2025",
    which is FALSE - it was vetoed).


  [C-6b] NO ENFORCEABLE CAP ON NONECONOMIC DAMAGES

    Fla. Stat. s. 766.118 still LISTS caps (including the $500,000 practitioner
    limit), but they are unenforceable:
      - Estate of McCall v. United States (Fla. 2014) - wrongful death
        noneconomic cap held unconstitutional under the Florida Constitution's
        equal protection guarantee.
      - North Broward Hospital District v. Kalitan (Fla. 2017) - extended the
        holding to personal injury (surviving patient) noneconomic caps.
      - Rationale: arbitrary reduction of compensation without regard to the
        severity of the injury bears no rational relationship to the
        Legislature's stated interest in addressing a malpractice crisis.

    STATUS: Florida is treated as a NO-CAP jurisdiction for ordinary medical
    negligence noneconomic damages as of 2026.

    WHY THIS IS A PAGE DEFECT: This is a strongly favorable fact for the reader
    and a high-intent query ("is there a cap on medical malpractice damages in
    Florida"). The page says nothing. Note the caps remain in the printed
    statute, so a reader who looks up s. 766.118 unaided will reach the WRONG
    conclusion - the page should preempt that.

[C-7] COMPARATIVE NEGLIGENCE - MED-MAL CARVE-OUT (not currently on page)
Severity: LOW (opportunity if the section is expanded)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FINDING: 2023 HB 837 moved Florida from pure to modified comparative negligence
  with a 51% bar - BUT Fla. Stat. s. 768.81(6) expressly EXEMPTS actions for
  personal injury or wrongful death arising out of medical negligence under
  Chapter 766.

  => Medical malpractice claims remain under PURE comparative negligence. A
     patient found 60% at fault can still recover 40% of damages.

  This is widely gotten wrong on competitor pages that apply the 51% bar
  site-wide. If a comparative-fault section is added here, this carve-out is a
  differentiator. Do NOT copy the 51%-bar language used on the firm's
  non-med-mal injury pages onto this page.

16.1  THE PROCESS FAILURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Section 15 added 16 URLs to sitemap.xml. They were validated ONLY by reading
  each page's on-disk canonical tag and confirming the sitemap entry matched it.

  That check answers "does this URL match what the file claims its canonical is".
  It does NOT answer "does this URL resolve". Those are different questions, and
  the second is the one a sitemap makes a promise about.

  NOT ONE of the 16 was ever requested against the running application. The
  validation block in 15.4 reads as though the work was verified - "All 16 new
  URLs match their page's on-disk canonical exactly ... TRUE" - which is a true
  statement about a check that does not establish what it appears to establish.
  That is the worst kind of green tick: accurate, and misleading.

  The user caught it. Testing then showed three pages were dead in BOTH
  languages - texting-and-driving, commercial-vehicle and railroad - so six of
  the sixteen URLs were 404s. Submitting known-404s in a sitemap is actively
  harmful, not merely useless: it spends crawl budget and signals low quality.

16.3  THE RULE THAT CAME OUT OF IT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  ANY SITEMAP ADDITION MUST BE HTTP-TESTED AGAINST A RUNNING INSTANCE BEFORE IT
  IS CONSIDERED DONE. A matching canonical tag is necessary, not sufficient.

  Generalised: do not report a thing as verified when what was actually checked
  is a proxy for it. Name the check that was run, not the conclusion hoped for.

  This rule has been applied in every pass since:

  WebFetch disagree on bot-protected hosts, and a `while read | curl` loop
  silently returns 000 because curl eats the loop's stdin. Never call a URL dead
  on one tool's reading.
